STRAW+10 GUIDE

Understanding the STRAW+10 Framework

What the STRAW+10 reproductive ageing framework is, its broad stages and limitations, and why MenoBrief does not calculate or assign a STRAW+10 stage.

What is STRAW+10?

STRAW+10 stands for the Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop + 10. It is an internationally recognised framework for describing the stages of reproductive ageing, including the transition through perimenopause and menopause. It was published in 2012 as an update to the original 2001 STRAW staging system.

STRAW+10 provides a standardised way to classify reproductive ageing from the late reproductive years through the menopausal transition into postmenopause. It is used in research and clinical contexts to improve consistency when discussing ovarian status.

The broad stages

STRAW+10 centres on the final menstrual period (FMP), which is Stage 0. The stages are grouped into three broad phases:

  • Reproductive phase: Stages –5 (early), –4 (peak) and –3 (late), leading up to the final menstrual period.
  • Menopausal transition: Stage –2 (early transition) and Stage –1 (late transition).
  • Postmenopause: Stage +1 (early postmenopause) and Stage +2 (late postmenopause).

Perimenopause is a general term still in common use. In STRAW+10 terms it broadly covers the menopausal transition (Stages –2 and –1) and ends 12 months after the final menstrual period.

How staging works

STRAW+10 uses menstrual cycle bleeding criteria as the most important basis for staging. Supportive biomarkers (such as FSH, AMH and antral follicle count) may be used where helpful but are not required, because assays are not standardised across laboratories.

Examples of the menstrual criteria include:

  • Stage –2 (early transition): a persistent difference of 7 or more days in the length of consecutive menstrual cycles;
  • Stage –1 (late transition): skipped cycles or absence of periods (amenorrhea) of 60 days or longer;
  • Stage +1a: 12 months after the final menstrual period.

STRAW+10 does not use age as a criterion for staging, and it is intended to apply regardless of age, ethnicity, body size or lifestyle characteristics.

Limitations

STRAW+10 is a research and clinical classification framework, not a diagnostic test. Important limitations include:

  • It does not diagnose menopause or perimenopause on its own;
  • People who have had a hysterectomy or endometrial ablation cannot be staged by menstrual bleeding criteria;
  • People under 40 with suspected premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) do not fit the model easily;
  • Symptoms are subjective, are not experienced by everyone, and there is no universal menopausal syndrome across all ethnic groups;
  • Important knowledge gaps remain, particularly for low-resource settings and some health conditions.

Staging should be interpreted by a qualified healthcare professional in the context of your overall health.

MenoBrief does not assign a STRAW+10 stage

MenoBrief is an appointment preparation and communication tool. It organises the symptoms, history and concerns you enter into a briefing PDF to support a conversation with a healthcare professional.

MenoBrief does not calculate, determine or assign a STRAW+10 stage. It does not perform clinical staging or diagnosis. Any assessment of reproductive stage should be made by a qualified healthcare professional based on your individual circumstances.
